# Syllabus · Italian for English speakers (A1 → B1)

Track folder: `content/it-for-en/` · Site path: `/learn-italian/` · Interface language: English

## How this syllabus was built

- **Goals come from the public CEFR descriptors** (Common European Framework, Companion Volume, 2020): each unit targets specific A1, A2 or B1 "can-do" statements for spoken interaction, spoken production, listening, reading and writing. The level checkpoints (units 8, 16, 24) test those descriptors directly.
- **Grammar is placed where a goal needs it**, not in textbook order. Example: the passato prossimo with *avere* arrives in unit 8 because the learner must say what they have done at the comune; *essere* + agreement arrives in unit 9 because the story needs "sono arrivato / arrivata".
- **Vocabulary is chosen by frequency and usefulness**: core function words and the most frequent 1,000 lemmas are front-loaded in A1; each unit then adds one theme set (25–40 words) tied to a real situation the protagonist lives through. Nothing is sequenced after any published course.
- **Every unit uses the fixed template**: La storia · Parole utili · Come funziona · Pronuncia · Tocca a te (25–40 exercises, ending with a mixed review) · Vivere lì · In sintesi.
- **Originality**: all characters, places, dialogues, readings and exercises are written for this project. Roccamarina is a fictional town so that streets, shops and offices can be invented freely; the "Vivere lì" sections describe real Italy. See `PROVENANCE.md`.

## The story: Luca / Lucia goes to Italy

**Protagonist.** Luca or Lucia Ferrante, 30, born and raised in Toronto. Grandparents (nonna Rosa and nonno Cosimo) emigrated from Roccamarina in 1962. Has Italian citizenship by descent, a mechanical bent and a plan: open a small bicycle repair-and-rental shop by the sea, find a home, and learn who the family was before Canada. Arrives knowing "ciao", "nonna" and the names of twelve pasta shapes.

**Setting.** Roccamarina (fictional), a mid-sized town on the Adriatic coast of Puglia: a centro storico of pale stone, a Corso with three bars, a Saturday market, a railway station on the regional line, olive groves inland, a lungomare that fills up every evening at seven. Later units travel to Napoli, Roma, Bologna, the Dolomiti and Sicilia.

**Cast (all original).**

| Character | Role | Voice in the story |
|---|---|---|
| **Dora Caputo**, 68 | Landlady, widow, owns the building on Via delle Rose; rents the protagonist the flat upstairs | Warm, bossy, feeds everyone, speaks fast, mixes in dialect |
| **Chiara Ferrante**, 27 | Cousin the protagonist has never met; pastry chef at Pasticceria Sant'Oronzo; dreams of a year abroad (she is the protagonist of the English track) | Curious, direct, the protagonist's guide to the family and the town |
| **Nando Russo**, 54 | Owner of Bar del Corso; knows everyone and every piece of news | Jokes, nicknames, teaches bar Italian and local opinion |
| **Tommaso Greco**, 34 | Mechanic who becomes the shop's partner | Practical, football-obsessed, patient with the protagonist's mistakes |
| **Pasquale Leone**, 72 | Downstairs neighbour, retired teacher, opinions on everything including your grammar | Pedantic, generous, the voice of "proper" Italian and of political debate |
| **Antonella Moro** | Official at the comune (anagrafe), later at the SUAP business desk | Precise, formal Lei, "manca un documento" |
| **Franco & Teresa Ferrante** | The protagonist's parents in Toronto (voice calls; they host Chiara in the English track) | Italo-Canadian English and Italian mixed |
| **Sofia Ferrante**, 22 | Younger sister in Toronto (main guide in the English track) | Appears in calls and the Christmas crossover |

**Crossover episodes** with the English track are marked ⇄: the same events are told from the other side of the ocean in the other track.

## Gender-aware design

Every episode, example and exercise involving the protagonist exists in a Luca version and a Lucia version, stored as variants (`{"m": "...", "f": "..."}`), never as duplicated files. Differences are highlighted automatically and, from unit 1 on, a short "why" note explains the agreement. Learners can toggle "show both versions". For exercises about themselves, learners set their own form (masculine, feminine or show both).

## Level map

| Level | Units | CEFR focus |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | 1–8 | Survival: introduce yourself, handle shops/café/directions, simple present-tense exchanges, first past tense |
| A2 | 9–16 | Routine life: narrate past events, describe habits and places, plan, make requests and complaints, first subjunctive |
| B1 | 17–24 | Independent life: narrate complex pasts, argue opinions, report speech, hypothesize, handle official texts, speak at social events |

## A1 · Arrivare (Units 1–8)

### Unit 1 · Benvenuto a Roccamarina / Benvenuta a Roccamarina
- **Story:** The protagonist steps off the regional train. Dora is on the platform holding a sign with the name misspelled. First words, first coffee, the flat on Via delle Rose. ⇄ (mirrors EN unit 1: Chiara's arrival at Pearson)
- **Goal (A1):** Greet and say goodbye; introduce yourself; say where you are from and your nationality; spell your name; understand and use numbers 0–20; ask simple questions.
- **Grammar:** Subject pronouns (and when to drop them) · *essere* (full present) · *chiamarsi* (mi chiamo / ti chiami / si chiama) · Gender of nouns (-o / -a, first -e) · Definite article singular (il, lo, la, l') · Nationality adjectives (-o/-a, -e) · Yes/no questions by intonation · Negation with *non* · *tu* vs *Lei* · First agreement note: *Benvenuto / Benvenuta*, *stanco / stanca*.
- **Vocabulary:** Greetings and politeness · Countries and nationalities · Personal data (nome, cognome, città, indirizzo) · Numbers 0–20 · The alphabet and "come si scrive".
- **Pronunciation:** The seven vowel sounds (a, e/è, i, o/ò, u) and why English speakers must not glide them · Stress on the penultimate syllable by default · Written accents (città, caffè, perché).
- **Living there:** Ciao vs buongiorno vs salve · tu and Lei in real life · Handshakes, kisses and personal space · What "Italian-Canadian" means in Italy.

### Unit 2 · La casa di Dora
- **Story:** The flat: two rooms, a terrace, a bidet, no dryer. Dora explains the rules, the moka, the condominio, and that the water heater has moods.
- **Goal (A1):** Describe a home and say what is where; say what you have and what there is; ask "what is this?" and "where is…?"; use numbers 20–100.
- **Grammar:** *avere* (full present) · *c'è / ci sono* · Indefinite article (un, uno, una, un') · Regular plurals (-o→-i, -a→-e, -e→-i) · Definite article plural (i, gli, le) · Question words *dove, che cosa, chi* · Basic adjectives and agreement (grande, piccolo, nuovo, vecchio) · *molto* as adverb · Numbers 20–100.
- **Vocabulary:** Rooms, furniture, household objects · Basic adjectives · "C'è un problema": things that break.
- **Pronunciation:** Hard and soft *c* and *g* (casa, cucina, giardino, gelato, chi, ghiaccio).
- **Living there:** Renting in Italy: contratto, caparra, spese condominiali · Italian homes: bidet, shutters, no dryer, the moka, sorting rubbish · Noise and quiet hours.

### Unit 3 · Al Bar del Corso
- **Story:** Nando's bar at 7:30. The protagonist orders a cappuccino at the wrong counter, pays at the wrong moment and gets a nickname ("il canadese / la canadese"). Nando explains how a bar works.
- **Goal (A1):** Order food and drink; ask for and understand prices; pay; say what you like and prefer; use numbers to 1,000 and the euro.
- **Grammar:** Regular *-are* verbs, full present (parlare, mangiare, pagare, lavorare, abitare) with spelling notes (-care/-gare, -ciare/-giare) · *vorrei* and *prendo* as fixed forms · Question words *quanto, cosa, quale* · *mi piace / mi piacciono* as fixed phrases · Numbers 100–1,000+ and prices · Adverbs *anche, solo, ancora*.
- **Vocabulary:** Bar food and drink · Colazione, pranzo, cena · Money and paying · Ordering phrases.
- **Pronunciation:** Double consonants, the number one problem for English speakers: *pala / palla, nono / nonno, sete / sette, caro / carro, fato / fatto*.
- **Living there:** Scontrino first, then order · Standing at the bancone vs sitting (and the price difference) · Cappuccino hours · The "caffè sospeso" · Breakfast is small and sweet.

### Unit 4 · La famiglia Ferrante
- **Story:** Chiara appears at the shop with a tray of pasticciotti and a family tree drawn on a napkin: who nonno Cosimo was, who is still here, who left. The protagonist describes the Toronto side.
- **Goal (A1):** Talk about family, age and jobs; describe people physically and by character; say what someone does.
- **Grammar:** Possessive adjectives (mio, tuo, suo…) and the family-noun rule (mia madre / la mia mamma / i miei genitori) · Adjective agreement in full (-o/-a/-i/-e, -e/-i) · Regular *-ere* and *-ire* verbs, full present, incl. *-isc-* (finire, capire, preferire) · *fare* (present) and *fare il / la* + job · *quanti anni hai?* · Adjective position basics.
- **Vocabulary:** Family (extended, Italian style) · Jobs and workplaces · Physical description and character adjectives.
- **Pronunciation:** *gn* (bagno, cognome) and *gli* (famiglia, figlio, moglie); the *sci / sce* sound.
- **Living there:** Italian families and the extended network · Italian emigration to Canada (post-war waves, Toronto, Montreal) · Names, nicknames and saints' days · How to say "Italian-Canadian" without offending anyone.

### Unit 5 · In giro per il paese
- **Story:** Chiara's hand-drawn map of Roccamarina; the protagonist gets lost looking for the ferramenta, asks three people and gets three answers; finds the comune, the station, the sea.
- **Goal (A1):** Ask for and give directions; name places in town; tell the time; ask about opening hours; say where you are going and how.
- **Grammar:** Irregular present: *andare, venire, stare, uscire, dare* · Simple prepositions (a, in, da, di, con, per, su, tra/fra) · Articulated prepositions (al, alla, allo, all', dal, nel, sul, del…) · Prepositions of place (davanti a, dietro, accanto a, di fronte a, vicino a, lontano da) · Time: *che ore sono?*, *a che ora?* · Days of the week · Imperative *tu* forms as fixed directions (gira, prendi, va' dritto) · *c'è un… qui vicino?*
- **Vocabulary:** Places in town · Directions · Means of transport (a piedi, in bici, in macchina, in treno) · Time and days.
- **Pronunciation:** The Italian *r*: single flap and double trill (*caro / carro, Roma, treno, arrivare*).
- **Living there:** The passeggiata and the piazza · Opening hours and the afternoon break · Sundays and Mondays · Signs you will see (chiuso per ferie, vietato, uscita).

### Unit 6 · Una settimana a Roccamarina
- **Story:** The first ordinary week: Dora's radio at six, the market on Saturday, the beach in the evening, a video call home. The protagonist's routine vs the town's routine. The first hot scirocco.
- **Goal (A1):** Describe daily routine and habits; say how often; talk about days, months, dates and the weather; say what you can, must and want to do.
- **Grammar:** Reflexive verbs (alzarsi, svegliarsi, lavarsi, vestirsi, divertirsi) · *dovere, potere, volere* (present) + infinitive · *sapere* (present) + infinitive · Frequency adverbs (sempre, spesso, di solito, qualche volta, mai) and *-mente* adverbs · Months, seasons, dates (il primo maggio, il 3 giugno) · Weather expressions (fa caldo, c'è il sole, piove) · *preferire*.
- **Vocabulary:** Daily actions · Parts of the day · Months, seasons, weather · Free-time activities.
- **Pronunciation:** Voiced and voiceless *s* (casa, rosa / sole, sera) · The two *z* sounds (zio, pizza / zero, zona).
- **Living there:** Italian daily rhythms: breakfast standing, long lunch, late dinner in the south · Market day · Beach culture: lido vs spiaggia libera, the ombrellone by the month · Ferragosto.

### Unit 7 · Al mercato del sabato
- **Story:** Dora sends the protagonist to the market with a list in her handwriting. Negotiating quantities, choosing tomatoes by touch, being told "non si toccano", buying a shirt for the festa patronale.
- **Goal (A1):** Buy food by weight and quantity; buy clothes by size and colour; say what you like and dislike properly; point to things; compare prices.
- **Grammar:** Partitive articles (del, della, dei, delle…) and *un po' di, qualche* · Quantities (un chilo di, un etto, una bottiglia di, un pacco di) · *piacere* in full with indirect pronouns (mi, ti, gli, le, ci, vi, gli) and *piace / piacciono* · *questo* and *quello* as adjectives · Colours and invariable adjectives (blu, rosa, viola) · All plural patterns: -co/-go, -ca/-ga, -io, -cia/-gia, invariable nouns, nouns in -à/-ù, foreign words · *quanto costa / costano*.
- **Vocabulary:** Fruit, vegetables, bread, cheese, meat, fish · Clothes, sizes, colours · Shops (alimentari, panificio, macelleria, ferramenta).
- **Pronunciation:** *sc* before e/i (pesce, scendere) vs *sc* before a/o/u (scuola) · *ch* and *gh* (chilo, spaghetti, ghiaccio).
- **Living there:** How a market works: don't touch, take a number at the banco · Bread by weight, cheese by the etto · Saldi season and shop hours · "Posso dare un'occhiata?" in a shop.

### Unit 8 · All'ufficio anagrafe · A1 checkpoint
- **Story:** The comune. Dottoressa Moro, a numbered ticket, a form in triplicate, a missing marca da bollo. The protagonist explains what they have already done (ho compilato, ho portato, ho pagato) and makes an appointment to come back.
- **Goal (A1 → A2):** Give personal details formally; fill in a form; make and change an appointment; say what you have already done; understand simple instructions; use dates and ordinals.
- **Grammar:** Passato prossimo with *avere*: regular participles (-ato, -uto, -ito) and the first irregulars (fatto, detto, preso, messo, visto, letto, scritto, aperto, chiuso) · *già / non… ancora* · Ordinal numbers and dates in full · *da quanto tempo…? da…* · Formal *Lei*: pronoun, verb forms, *Scusi / Prego / Mi dica* · The formal imperative as fixed forms (si accomodi, firmi qui).
- **Vocabulary:** Documents (codice fiscale, carta d'identità, residenza, tessera sanitaria, certificato) · Forms (nome, cognome, luogo e data di nascita, stato civile) · Office language (sportello, modulo, appuntamento, orario di apertura).
- **Pronunciation:** Stress and written accents revisited: *è / e, dà / da, perché, città, venerdì*; stress in verb forms (*parlano*, not *parlàno*).
- **Living there:** The codice fiscale and why you need it for everything · Iscrizione anagrafica and residency · Marca da bollo, fotocopie, the queue ticket · Citizenship by descent (iure sanguinis) in a nutshell · Patience as a skill.
- **Checkpoint:** A1 self-test across the four skills; mixed review of units 1–8.

## A2 · Vivere (Units 9–16)

### Unit 9 · Com'è andata?
- **Story:** Sunday lunch at the family's: twelve relatives, four courses, one question repeated: "E com'è andata la prima settimana?" The protagonist narrates: sono arrivato / arrivata, ho conosciuto, mi sono perso / persa, siamo andati al mare. Nonno Cosimo's house, found at last.
- **Goal (A2):** Narrate past events in order; say when they happened; talk about what you have and have never done; tell a short anecdote.
- **Grammar:** Passato prossimo with *essere*: which verbs, participle agreement (the Luca / Lucia difference made explicit: *sono arrivato / arrivata, siamo andati / andate*) · Reflexive and reciprocal verbs in the past (*mi sono alzato/a, ci siamo conosciuti*) · More irregular participles (stato, venuto, rimasto, nato, morto, vissuto, piaciuto, successo) · Time expressions (ieri, l'altro ieri, la settimana scorsa, due giorni fa, nel 2019) · *mai, già, ancora, appena, sempre* placement · Double negation (non… mai, non… niente, non… nessuno).
- **Vocabulary:** Travel and movement verbs · Life events · Time markers · Food at a long family lunch.
- **Pronunciation:** Open and closed *e* and *o* (*pésca / pèsca, bótte / bòtte, vénti / vènti*) and why the south opens them.
- **Living there:** Sunday lunch · The Mezzogiorno: history, stereotypes and reality · Feste patronali and the patron saint · Memory of emigration: the ships to Halifax, Pier 21.

### Unit 10 · Cerco un locale
- **Story:** Looking for a shop space: a damp garage on the port, a bright but expensive shop on the Corso, a former barber's with a view. Phone calls with owners, a visit with Tommaso (met through Nando), comparing everything.
- **Goal (A2):** Describe and compare places; express preferences and needs; make phone calls and appointments; understand ads.
- **Grammar:** Comparatives (*più / meno… di / che*, *tanto… quanto*) and when to use *di* vs *che* · Relative superlative (il più grande del paese) and absolute superlative (*-issimo*, *molto*) · Irregular forms (migliore, peggiore, maggiore, minore; meglio, peggio) · *bello, buono, quello* before nouns · Direct object pronouns (mi, ti, lo, la, ci, vi, li, le) and placement · *ci* for places (ci vado, ci sono stato/a) · Possessive pronouns (il mio, la tua) · Phone formulas.
- **Vocabulary:** Premises, business basics (affitto, locale, vetrina, magazzino, contratto) · Adjectives for places · Phone language · Ads and abbreviations.
- **Pronunciation:** Question and statement intonation; phone intonation ("Pronto?").
- **Living there:** Starting a small business: partita IVA, the commercialista, Camera di Commercio, SCIA at the SUAP desk · Commercial leases · Why everyone says "chiedi a un commercialista".

### Unit 11 · Com'era Roccamarina
- **Story:** Dora's photo album and Pasquale's corrections: the town in 1962, the port, the school with one teacher, the day nonno Cosimo left. The protagonist describes childhood in Toronto: the snow, the Italian Saturday school, nonna's basement kitchen.
- **Goal (A2):** Describe the past: how things were, what people used to do; contrast then and now; describe childhood.
- **Grammar:** Imperfetto, full conjugation (regular; *essere, fare, dire, bere, tradurre, porre*) · Uses: habit, description, age, time, weather, ongoing background · Imperfetto vs passato prossimo: background vs event, *mentre* vs *quando* · *da bambino / da bambina* (variant) · *c'era una volta* · *stare per* + infinitive (past).
- **Vocabulary:** Childhood, school, games · Old-time objects and jobs · The sea and the port · Memory verbs (ricordare, sembrare).
- **Pronunciation:** Elision and apocope (l'amico, un'amica, un bel giorno, buon lavoro); linking words in fast speech.
- **Living there:** Southern Italy in the 1960s and the "valigia di cartone" · The Italian school system in brief · Italian Saturday schools abroad · Dialect vs Italian: what Dora speaks, what Pasquale corrects.

### Unit 12 · Dal medico
- **Story:** A fall from a test bike on the lungomare, a swollen wrist, Dora's remedies, the guardia medica, the pharmacist who is also Nando's cousin. Instructions, instructions, instructions.
- **Goal (A2):** Describe symptoms and pain; understand and give instructions and advice; buy medicine; say what you are doing right now.
- **Grammar:** Imperative *tu, noi, voi*: regular and irregular (va', fa', di', da', sta', sii, abbi) · Negative imperative (*non* + infinitive for tu) · Imperative with pronouns (dimmi, fallo, alzati, mettiti, non ti preoccupare / non preoccuparti) · *stare* + gerundio (sto male, sto prendendo) and gerundio formation · Body-part plurals (il braccio / le braccia, il dito / le dita, il ginocchio / le ginocchia, la mano / le mani) · *fare male* (mi fa male la testa / mi fanno male le gambe).
- **Vocabulary:** Body · Symptoms and illnesses · Medicines and the pharmacy · Doctor's office phrases.
- **Pronunciation:** Consonant clusters that English speakers soften (*sdraiati, stretto, sbagliato, psicologo*) and double consonants in instructions (*metti, smetti*).
- **Living there:** The Servizio Sanitario Nazionale: medico di base, tessera sanitaria, ticket · Farmacia di turno, the green cross · Guardia medica, pronto soccorso and triage colours · Grandmother medicine (correnti d'aria, the cervicale).

### Unit 13 · Apriamo!
- **Story:** Keys to the shop. Tommaso and the protagonist paint, argue about the name ("Ciclofficina Ferrante" vs "Bici & Mare"), plan the opening and promise Dora the first ride. Nando predicts rain, Pasquale predicts failure. ⇄ (EN unit 13: Chiara's first new product at the bakery)
- **Goal (A2):** Talk about plans, intentions and predictions; make promises; talk about the weather forecast; say what you will do if / when.
- **Grammar:** Futuro semplice, full conjugation (regular; spelling -care/-gare/-ciare/-giare; irregular stems: sarò, avrò, andrò, farò, verrò, vorrò, potrò, dovrò, saprò, vedrò, vivrò, rimarrò, berrò, terrò, darò, starò) · Future for probability (*sarà Dora*) · *se / quando* + futuro, futuro · Indirect object pronouns in full (mi, ti, gli, le, Le, ci, vi, gli / loro) · Stressed pronouns (a me, con te, per lui) · *piacere*-type verbs (mancare, servire, interessare, bastare, sembrare).
- **Vocabulary:** Work and the workshop · Tools and bike parts · Weather forecast · Plans and projects.
- **Pronunciation:** Final stress in the future (*-rò, -rà, -ranno*) and how it changes meaning (*parlo / parlò*).
- **Living there:** The inaugurazione and the local priest · Shop opening hours and seasonal closures · Bike culture: the Giro d'Italia, ciclabili, bici vs motorino in the south · Chiedere un preventivo.

### Unit 14 · Clienti e vicini
- **Story:** Opening week: a German tourist with a flat tyre, a nonna who wants a bike for her grandson, Pasquale who complains about the sign, the comune that wants a form for the sign. Polite requests, polite refusals, a first real complaint.
- **Goal (A2):** Handle service encounters; make polite requests, suggestions and complaints; give advice; express opinions; describe things with relative clauses.
- **Grammar:** Condizionale presente, full conjugation (regular; irregular: sarei, avrei, andrei, farei, vorrei, potrei, dovrei, saprei, verrei, rimarrei, berrei) · Uses: politeness, wishes, advice (*dovresti*), unconfirmed news · Imperative *Lei* (si accomodi, mi dica, scusi, non si preoccupi) and *Loro* for recognition · *ne* (quantity and "about it") · Combined pronouns (me lo, te la, glielo, ce ne) · Relative pronouns *che* and *cui* (with prepositions).
- **Vocabulary:** Shop service · Repairs and problems · Complaints and apologies · Opinions.
- **Pronunciation:** *gli* + vowel in pronoun clusters (*glielo, gliene*); sentence stress in polite requests.
- **Living there:** Customer culture in a small town: credit, trust, "paghi la prossima volta" · Shop signs, noise and the vigili urbani · Tourists vs locals in the south · The art of the polite complaint.

### Unit 15 · Weekend a Napoli
- **Story:** The protagonist and Chiara take the regionale to Bari and the Frecciarossa to Napoli: tickets, validation, a hostel, a pizzeria with a queue, the Metro Art stations, a scooter near-miss, the first sfogliatella. Chiara admits she has applied for a Canadian visa.
- **Goal (A2):** Book and buy travel; understand station announcements; order a full meal; describe a city and an experience; say how long things take.
- **Grammar:** Verb + preposition + infinitive (cominciare a, continuare a, riuscire a, imparare a, provare a, finire di, cercare di, smettere di, avere bisogno di, avere voglia di, decidere di) and verb + infinitive with no preposition (volere, potere, dovere, preferire, sapere) · *prima di* + infinitive, *dopo aver* + participle · *sapere* vs *conoscere* in all tenses · *volerci* vs *metterci* · Passato prossimo of modal verbs (ho dovuto prendere / sono dovuto/a andare) · Impersonal *si* intro (si mangia bene).
- **Vocabulary:** Trains and stations · Accommodation · Restaurant: courses, menu, paying · City life.
- **Pronunciation:** Dialect vs standard as listening practice (Neapolitan samples with standard rendering); the *non* reduction in fast speech.
- **Living there:** Trenitalia vs Italo, regionale vs alta velocità, validating tickets · Coperto, no tipping culture, the menu structure · Pizza napoletana and the AVPN · Regional Italy: 20 regions, many dialects, one television Italian.

### Unit 16 · Chiara parte · A2 checkpoint
- **Story:** The visa arrives. A farewell dinner at Dora's with the whole cast; toasts, advice, worries, promises to call. The protagonist writes Chiara a card in Italian. ⇄ (EN unit 1 begins where this ends)
- **Goal (A2 → B1):** Express feelings, hopes, wishes and doubts; give advice; make a toast; write an informal letter or email; talk about everyone and no one.
- **Grammar:** Congiuntivo presente intro: regular forms; *essere, avere, andare, fare, stare, dare, sapere*; after *penso che, credo che, spero che, voglio che, è importante che, è meglio che, bisogna che* · Indicative vs subjunctive: *so che* vs *penso che* · Indefinite pronouns and adjectives (qualcuno, nessuno, qualcosa, niente, ogni, ognuno, tutti, alcuni, qualche, chiunque) · Informal letter conventions (cara / caro, un abbraccio, a presto).
- **Vocabulary:** Feelings and emotions · Wishes and advice · Celebrations and toasts · Travel preparation.
- **Pronunciation:** Review of every difficult sound through tongue-twisters and a recorded self-check.
- **Living there:** Young Italians abroad and the "fuga dei cervelli" · The Italy–Canada youth mobility agreement (working holiday) · Farewell rituals: brindisi, "in bocca al lupo / crepi", the envelope · Italian emails: formal and informal openings and closings.
- **Checkpoint:** A2 self-test; mixed review of units 9–16.

## B1 · Restare (Units 17–24)

### Unit 17 · Il primo inverno
- **Story:** Told a year later, as a long flashback: the shop's first winter, when the tourists left, the rent was due, Tommaso had already found another job, and Nando had told everyone it was over. How it did not end. Pasquale's version vs Nando's version, and the newspaper article in the passato remoto.
- **Goal (B1):** Narrate a complex story with background, sequence and prior events; understand a written narrative and a news article; summarise what happened.
- **Grammar:** Trapassato prossimo (avevo già chiuso / ero già partito/a) · The three past tenses together (passato prossimo, imperfetto, trapassato) with connectors (*prima, dopo che, appena, ormai, intanto, nel frattempo*) · Passato remoto for recognition: regular endings and the key irregulars (fu, ebbe, fece, disse, andò, venne, vide, diede, stette, nacque, morì, scrisse, prese, mise, rimase, seppe, volle) · *stare per* and *avere appena* in the past.
- **Vocabulary:** Narrative connectors · Business ups and downs (incasso, spese, debiti, prestito) · Seasons in tourism · Newspaper language.
- **Pronunciation:** Rhythm and sentence stress in narrative; reading a paragraph aloud with pauses.
- **Living there:** Seasonal economies in the south: "mordi e fuggi" tourism · Italian newspapers and the passato remoto · Micro-credit and the banca locale · Resilience, southern style.

### Unit 18 · A tavola si discute
- **Story:** Pasquale invites the protagonist and Tommaso to dinner. Three hours on football, the government, young people, the north, the south, "i canadesi" and whether the bike lane on the Corso is a plot. The protagonist learns to disagree without losing the dessert.
- **Goal (B1):** Give, justify and defend opinions; agree and disagree politely; express doubt, necessity and possibility; follow a lively discussion.
- **Grammar:** Congiuntivo presente consolidated: all irregulars (possa, voglia, debba, sappia, venga, esca, beva, dica, faccia, rimanga, tenga, scelga, ponga, traduca, muoia) · Impersonal expressions + subjunctive (è possibile che, è probabile che, è strano che, può darsi che) · Conjunctions that require the subjunctive (benché, sebbene, nonostante, affinché, perché = so that, purché, a condizione che, a meno che non, prima che, senza che) · *credo di sì / credo che* · *secondo me, a mio parere* · Opinion verbs with indicative (*so, sono sicuro che*).
- **Vocabulary:** Opinions and argument · Society and politics basics · Sport (calcio) · Debate connectors.
- **Pronunciation:** Emphasis and contrast; irony intonation ("Ma certo!"); overlapping speech as listening practice.
- **Living there:** The Italian conversation style: interruptions, hands, volume ≠ anger · Calcio as identity · Politics at the table, and when not · The bar as parliament.

### Unit 19 · Una videochiamata da Toronto
- **Story:** Chiara calls from the Ferrantes' basement in a parka, two months in: the job, the snow, Sofia, the streetcar that never came. The protagonist relays everything to Dora and Nando, who have questions. The protagonist realises they miss Toronto and belong here, at once. ⇄ (EN unit 11 and 16)
- **Goal (B1):** Report what someone said, asked or requested; relay news and messages; express surprise, nostalgia and doubt about the past; compare lives.
- **Grammar:** Discorso indiretto: *ha detto che* + tense shifts (presente → imperfetto, passato prossimo → trapassato, futuro → condizionale passato intro), *ha chiesto se / quando / dove*, *ha detto di* + infinitive for commands · Congiuntivo passato (*penso che abbia trovato lavoro, credo che sia arrivata*) · *mancare* in full (mi manchi, ci mancano) · *sembrare che* + subjunctive · Demonstratives and time/place shifts in reported speech (questo → quello, oggi → quel giorno).
- **Vocabulary:** Communication and technology · Emigration and distance · Nostalgia · Canadian life from an Italian viewpoint (loanwords and explanations).
- **Pronunciation:** Fast-speech reductions (*c'ho, vabbè, 'spetta, 'sto*); recognising them without imitating them.
- **Living there:** Italians abroad today: AIRE, the consulate, voting from abroad · Italo-Canadian "italiese" (garbiccio, basamento, storo) · Keeping in touch across six time zones · Nonna on video.

### Unit 20 · Se avessi una casa mia
- **Story:** Dora announces she wants to sell the building to her nephew in Milano, who would turn it into B&Bs. Unless… The protagonist and Dora imagine deals, loans, parents' opinions. Hypotheticals at the kitchen table. The notaio's cousin is Nando.
- **Goal (B1):** Make hypotheses about unreal present situations; express wishes and regrets; talk about buying and financing a home; negotiate.
- **Grammar:** Congiuntivo imperfetto, full conjugation (regular; *fossi, avessi, facessi, dicessi, bevessi, stessi, dessi, traducessi, ponessi*) · Periodo ipotetico type 1 (realtà) and type 2 (possibilità: *se avessi… comprerei*) · *magari* + congiuntivo imperfetto · *come se* + congiuntivo imperfetto · *vorrei che* + congiuntivo imperfetto · Sequence of tenses with the subjunctive, part 1 (main verb in present vs past).
- **Vocabulary:** Buying property (notaio, rogito, compromesso, mutuo, caparra, catasto) · Money and banks · Decisions and dreams.
- **Pronunciation:** Vowel length in stressed open syllables (*fa-re* vs *fat-to*); doubling across word boundaries (raddoppiamento sintattico: *a casa* → "accasa") as a listening phenomenon.
- **Living there:** Buying a house in Italy: agenzia, proposta, compromesso, rogito, notaio costs · Mutui and the "prima casa" · The one-euro houses · The family home and inheritance culture.

### Unit 21 · In bici verso nord
- **Story:** A spring trip: a bike fair in Bologna, then the Dolomiti with Tommaso. A broken chain in the Apennines, a hotel that lost the booking, a conversation in Alto Adige that starts in German. - **Goal (B1):** Describe experiences in detail; plan an itinerary and talk about completed future actions; handle problems and complaints when travelling; express regret and what should have been; understand regional varieties.
- **Grammar:** Condizionale passato (avrei dovuto portare, sarei dovuto/a partire prima, saremmo arrivati/e — variants) and its use for the "future in the past" · Futuro anteriore (quando avrò finito, sarà già partito/a) · *appena, non appena, finché non* · Relative pronouns extended (*il quale / la quale, chi, ciò che, quello che, il cui*) · Suffixes (-ino, -etto, -one, -accio, -issimo revisited).
- **Vocabulary:** Cycling and mountains · Repairs and emergencies · Hotels and complaints · North / south vocabulary differences (lexical variants).
- **Pronunciation:** Northern vs southern accents: *s* between vowels, open/closed vowels, double consonants, the Bolognese *s*; listening to four regional speakers.
- **Living there:** North–South: economy, stereotypes and the questione meridionale in two paragraphs · Emilia food · Alto Adige, German and the autonomia · Driving in Italy: ZTL, autovelox, tutor, the autostrada · Cycling tourism and the ciclovie.

### Unit 22 · Carte, contratti e burocrazia
- **Story:** The shop hires its first seasonal employee: contracts, INPS, the commercialista's emails, a PEC that must be activated, a notice from the comune in pure officialese. The protagonist learns to read "si comunica che" and to write "in attesa di un Suo cortese riscontro".
- **Goal (B1):** Understand formal letters, notices and contracts; write a formal email; deal with public offices and employment paperwork; describe processes impersonally.
- **Grammar:** Passive voice: *essere* + participle in all tenses, *venire* + participle (processes), *andare* + participle (obligation: *va compilato*) · Agent with *da* · *si passivante* (si vendono biciclette, si affitta) and *si impersonale* (si dice, si mangia bene, *si è stanchi* and the plural-adjective rule) · *si* with reflexive verbs (ci si alza) · Formal register: openings, closings, nominalisations, "Le / La" capitalised · Abbreviations.
- **Vocabulary:** Contracts and work (contratto a tempo determinato / indeterminato, busta paga, ferie, malattia, TFR) · Bureaucracy (PEC, SPID, INPS, INAIL, IVA, raccomandata, autocertificazione) · Formal email language.
- **Pronunciation:** Reading official text aloud; pronouncing acronyms (INPS, IVA, PEC, ASL, ISEE); stress in long formal words.
- **Living there:** Types of work contracts and the busta paga · SPID and PEC: the two keys to the Italian state · The commercialista, again · How to write to a public office and actually get an answer.

### Unit 23 · Un matrimonio in Sicilia
- **Story:** Tommaso's sister marries in Siracusa: ferry, forty relatives, a lunch of eleven courses, the busta, a speech the protagonist must give in Italian. What they would have said if they had known the bride's ex would be there. Idioms fly; the protagonist understands most of them.

- **Grammar:** Congiuntivo trapassato (se avessi saputo, se fossi arrivato/a) · Periodo ipotetico type 3 (*se avessi saputo, sarei venuto/a prima*) and mixed types · Full sequence of tenses (concordanza dei tempi) with indicative and subjunctive, as a reference table · Gerundio passato (avendo finito, essendo arrivato/a) and *pur* + gerundio · Infinito passato (dopo aver mangiato, prima di essere partito/a) · Idioms with body parts and food.
- **Vocabulary:** Weddings and ceremonies · Emotions, nuanced · Idioms and proverbs · Sicilian food and places.
- **Pronunciation:** Formal, slow, clear speech for a toast; Sicilian sounds (cacuminal *dd*, vowel system) as listening contrast.
- **Living there:** Italian weddings: bomboniere, the busta, the confetti, the lunch that never ends · Civil vs religious ceremonies · Sicily in two pages · Regional dialects as identity, not error.
